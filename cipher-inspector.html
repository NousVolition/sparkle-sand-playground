How to use this
  1. Press Start. A built-in test cipher is already loaded, so you can try it straight away. You can also paste your own cipher text into the box first.
  2. Watch the cyan line in the chart under the colored grid. When it climbs into the shaded band, the decoded text is starting to look like English.
  3. The amber line is the ‘heat’. It starts high, so the solver tries wild guesses, then cools, so it settles on the best ones it has found.
  4. A run takes about 2 minutes at Normal speed on a typical screen (about 4 minutes at Careful, about 1 minute at Fast) and stops by itself. The progress bar and ‘time left’ line show the live estimate.
  5. If the cyan line goes flat outside the band (the state line then reads ‘looks stuck’), press Reheat, or choose a new seed and press Reset, then Start.
  6. When it finishes, read the Answer check on the right. It checks the result with separate code, and the decoded text appears above it.
  7. Every small ? explains the item next to it. Point at it, tap it, or reach it with the Tab key.

Wirtinger roughness gauge. Each window of the last n frames is centered and treated as a cycle (the last point wraps to the first). R = Σ(f(j+1) − f(j))² / Σ f(j)² and λ₁ = 2 − 2cos(2π/n), so W = R / λ₁. For a nonconstant window with n ≥ 3, W ≥ 1 follows from the discrete Wirtinger inequality on ZMod n. Lean proof of the bound ↗ · Cosine equality case ↗ (cos(2πj/n), n ≥ 3). Every frame checks R ≥ λ₁ − 10⁻¹² in the console. W near 1 needs a window shaped like one smooth wave; a steady climb gives roughly W ≈ 0.3n because of the wrap, and frame-to-frame noise gives about 2/λ₁ (≈ 730 for n = 120). These Lean proofs concern real-valued cycles. The browser calculations, solver convergence and decoded text are outside their scope.

How the view is made. The cipher fills a rows × columns grid row by row, and the grid is read by the stride: each step moves dx columns right and dy rows down, wrapping at the edges. When the stride comes back to a cell it has already read (this happens when the stride and the grid size share a factor), the reading jumps to the first unread cell in row order, so every cell is read exactly once. Each pair of neighbors in that reading is drawn as a curved chord: gold when it crosses between the rings, cyan when it stays on one ring. Sukhotin's vowel algorithm splits the symbols into an inner ring (likely vowels) and an outer ring (likely consonants); it counts neighbors only along the unrolled reading order. The split is an estimate, not an identification, and on a homophonic cipher it is weaker, because each letter is spread over several symbols. The reference rings and curved chords are a display only. Folds = 17 matches the 17 columns of the Z340 grid; folds = 30 equals the Coxeter number of E₈, but here that is only a display choice with no link between the cipher and E₈. The stride dx = 2, dy = 1 is similar to the stride used in the 2020 Z340 solve by Oranchak, Blake and Van Eycke, which read two of the cipher's three sections along a diagonal; it is not the same transposition. The Z340 grid in the test list is the Webtoy transcription from the Zodiac Killer Ciphers Wiki (cross-checked against zkdecrypto); with a 20 × 17 grid, folds = 17 matches its width.

The 20 × 17 Z340 grid (Webtoy transcription, Zodiac Killer Ciphers Wiki) sits on a torus, R = 135, tube radius 58: the column gives the angle around the hole and the row the angle around the tube.
